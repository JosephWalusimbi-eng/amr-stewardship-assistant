FOREWORD
Dr. Aceng Jane Ruth Ocero
Hon. Minister of Health
Pharmaceuticals play a very critical role in achieving 
the overall outcomes and goals related to the health 
sector. The National Health Policy, the National 
Medicines Policy, the Health Sector Strategic and 
Investment Plan III, and the National Pharmaceutical 
Sector Strategic Plan 2015-2020 emphasize the 
promotion of appropriate use of medicines, health and 
laboratory supplies.
Appropriate use of medicines requires good diagnostic 
skills and access to well-performed laboratory 
services, good prescribing practices that follow clinical 
guidelines, good dispensing practices, and adherence 
to treatment by the patient. Very importantly, 
appropriate use requires the availability of essential 
medicines and health supplies (EMHS) at every service 
delivery point, combined with an efficient monitoring 
and evaluation system.
The Essential Medicines and Health Supplies List for 
Uganda (EMHSLU) 2023 reflects the policy of the 
Ministry of Health, for the appropriate procurement 
of safe and efficacious essential medicines, health and 
laboratory supplies in public institutions.
Like in the previous editions, all items have been 
categorised by the level of care, and also by the Vital, 
Essential and Necessary (VEN) classification. The VEN 
system prioritises items according to health impact, 
to ensure that adequate stock levels of life-saving 
medicines and health supplies at the facility will always 
be available, leading to better healthcare services for 
Ugandans.
The EMHSLU 2023 was compiled based on 
recommendations from various categories of health 
workers at the Ministry of Health and from health 
facilities. It was updated in conjunction with the 
Uganda Clinical Guidelines (UCG) 2023, to ensure that 
all essential medicines, health and laboratory supplies 
needed to treat common conditions according to the 
UCG are included.
It is the hope of the Ministry of Health that the 
updated essential medicines and health supplies 
list will be used by providers in both the public and 
private sectors, to guide, ensure availability and use of 
quality, safe, efficacious and cost-effective medicines, 
health and laboratory supplies in Uganda. 
I wish to thank all the members of the Ministry of 
Health Update Task Force, government parastatals, 
the medical consultants, district health workers, the 
private sector, the Uganda Reproductive Maternal 
Child and Adolescent Health Improvement Project 
(URMCHIP) and development partners. 
Finally, I thank the consultancy firm, Zenith Solutions 
Limited, that coordinated the overall inputs that led to 
the successful development of this book.
Dr. Aceng Jane Ruth Ocero
Hon. Minister of Health

The EMHSLU 2023 has maintained the previous 
arrangement of four sections i.e., essential medicines 
list, general health supplies list, specialist health 
supplies list, and the laboratory supplies list.
 The medicines list is classified by therapeutic 
categories, following the 22nd edition of the World 
Health Organisation (WHO) model list (2021), and 
contains both the general and specialist medicines in 
one list for ease of reference.
The laboratory list developed in consultation with 
UNHLS includes standardized equipment and supplies, 
harmonized with the test techniques for each level of 
care within the health system, so as to ensure access 
to high quality, rapid, and affordable diagnostic tests 
for the care, treatment, prevention and surveillance of 
the conditions affecting the Ugandan population.
In order to promote availability of medicines at 
all levels, all items in the EMHSLU 2023 have been 
classified by the “level of care” (L ) corresponding to 
the competencies available at those levels, and also 
by the Vital, Essential and Necessary (VEN) criteria. 
During procurement, the vital (V) should be given 
priority over the ‘essential’ and ‘necessary’, as these 
are lifesaving and/or first line recommendations for 
the conditions, based on the available budgetary 
allocations.
This fully updated publication replaces the EMHSLU 
2016 and is being circulated to all public and private 
sector prescribers, pharmacists, training institutions, 
procurement agencies, warehouses, and regulatory 
authorities in the country.
The Essential Medicines and Health Supplies List for 
Uganda (EMHSLU) 2023 is a list of safe, efficacious, 
and cost-effective medicines and health supplies 
that suit the healthcare needs of the majority of the 
Ugandan population.
Access to essential medicines and health supplies is 
fundamental to ensuring equitable healthcare to the 
population. In addition, availability of proper diagnostic 
supplies and technologies ensures the appropriate use 
of these medicines and guarantees the overall quality 
of care received by the patients. In a resource-limited 
setting, the judicious use of supplies is required, and 
this EMHSLU provides a basis for efficient supply chain 
management, planning and procurement of supplies by 
the health facilities and procurement agencies.
The EMHSLU 2023 follows the 2016 edition, and 
has been harmonized with the UCG 2023, so as to 
reflect medicines and diagnostic requirements for 
management of common conditions affecting the 
Ugandan population. Updating this edition was guided 
by a user survey that highlighted the usefulness, 
challenges and scope of the guidelines. This was

Essential Medicines Concept
In 1999, the World Health Organization Expert 
Committee on Essential Medicines defined essential 
medicines as those medicines that satisfy the healthcare 
needs of the majority of the population at a cost that 
is affordable to them and their community. Essential 
medicines should always be available, in adequate 
amounts, and in the appropriate dosage forms.
The essential medicines concept is a public health 
principle that promotes efficient use of resources by 
establishing and using a limited list of carefully selected 
medicines. By selecting a few medicines, better 
treatment and easier supply, storage, and distribution 
can be assured at a lower cost. The concept is based 
on the observations that:
• The vast majority of health problems for most 
of the population can be treated with a small 
number of carefully selected medicines.
• In practice, most doctors and other health 
professionals routinely use fewer than 200 
medicines; training and clinical experience should 
focus on the proper use of these medicines.
VEN classification
In the context of limited resources, it is very important 
to learn to prioritise medicines and health supplies. 
This is reflected by the Vital, Essential, Necessary 
(VEN) classification. Items are classified into three 
categories, according to their health impact:
V: Vital items are used to diagnose and treat 
life-threatening conditions, or are considered 
medicine of choice or “first line” items in their 
therapeutic category. Their unavailability would 
cause serious harm and side effects. They must 
be ALWAYS available.
E: Essential medicines are important; they 
are used to treat common illnesses, maybe 
less severe but nevertheless significant, or 
are second line items in their therapeutic 
categories.
N: Necessary (or sometimes called nonessential) medicines are those used for minor 
or self-limiting illnesses, or diseases with less 
impact on the population, or items with a high 
cost for marginal therapeutic benefit.
The VEN classification is intended to guide health 
facilities to prioritize items during procurement. Vital 
items have first priority, because their unavailability 
can lead to death of a patient or irreparable injury. 
Essential items have second priority; if these items are 
not available, the patient could suffer pain or great 
discomfort. Necessary items are needed and therefore 
on the order form; however, they have third priority.
The VEN-classified EMHSLU also guides the National 
Medical Stores on which items to procure and which 
to include in the kits.
VEN classification also allows for better and simplified 
stock, and storage management as a limited number 
of the most crucial items are available, which leads to 
better supply chain efficiency and effectiveness.
AWaRe classification
This applies to antimicrobial agents in relation to 
antimicrobial resistance. 
Products are classified in stewardship groups, namely, 
access, watch and reserve.
Access
Antibiotics with activities against commonly 
encountered susceptible pathogens and shows lower 
resistance potential than in other groups. 
Watch
Antibiotics that have higher resistance potential 
include most of the highest priority agents among the 
critically important anti-microbial agents. They should 
be sparingly used to avoid potential resistance. 
Reserve
These are antibiotics that should be reserved for 
treatment of confirmed or suspected infections due 
to multi-drug resistant organisms. Antibiotics in this 
group should be treated as last resort options. 
In this edition, effort has been made to avail them at a 
high specific level of care and patients.
Prescribers are encouraged to take the AWaRe 
classification into consideration when prescribing 
antimicrobial agents. Availability of antimicrobial 
agents should be made at levels of care depending on 
prescriber competences. Their prescription and use 
should be guided by laboratory results.
Level of use (L)
The items in the EMHSLU 2023 have also been 
grouped according to level of use. Each item has a 
designation representing the lowest level of use. The 
item can be prescribed and dispensed at this level and 
all the levels above. This designation is in line with the 
diagnostic and clinical skills expected to be available

STRUCTURE OF EMHSLU 
2023
The EMHSLU 2023 comprises four sections, A-D.
Section A: Essential Medicines List 2023
This comprises both general use and specialist 
medicines. These have been classified by therapeutic 
categories following a similar structure to the 19th 
WHO Model List of Essential Medicines, 2021. It 
consists of all medicines recommended in the UCG 
2023 that were selected based on efficacy, safety and 
cost-effectiveness.
The specialist medicines are those for which 
specialized medical care, training, diagnosis or 
monitoring is needed. These are mostly restricted 
to regional and national referral hospitals where the 
required expertise is available.
The relevant International Non-proprietary Name 
(INN) or generic names for the medicines are used 
throughout the list. Short forms of medicine names 
are used for only well-known abbreviations such AZT 
for zidovudine.
This section is presented in tables labeled as: 
DS=dosage form, STR = strength, L = Level of use, C 
= VEN classification.
Section B: General Health Supplies
This section includes medical sundries used in all 
healthcare facilities and are arranged in alphabetical 
order.
Section C: Specialist Health Supplies
This list includes supplies used in specialized situations 
such as ophthalmology, dialysis, etc.
Section D: Laboratory Health Supplies
The laboratory list includes standardized equipment 
and supplies, harmonized with the test techniques, 
operating procedures for each type of test and for 
each level of care within the health system. The 
standardization is meant to strengthen the country’s 
laboratory capacity and service delivery to provide 
access to high quality, rapid and affordable diagnostic 
tests for the care, treatment, prevention and 
surveillance of infectious and non-infectious diseases.
The standardized laboratory list, which is also VEN-
classified, helps to reduce the number of commodities 
being managed by the procurement bodies, leading to 
better supply chain management.
